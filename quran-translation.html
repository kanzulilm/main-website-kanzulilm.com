Understanding the Science

Tafseer vs Translation:
What is the Difference?

A Quran translation gives a basic linguistic equivalent of the text in another language — it tells you what the words mean. Quran Translation goes far deeper: it explains why each verse was revealed, its historical context (Shan-e-Nuzool), the specific occasion that prompted it (Asbab al-Nuzool), how it relates to other verses, what scholarly debates surround it, and what practical Islamic rulings are derived from it.

Reading a translation is like reading a summary of a book. Tafseer is studying the book itself — with a scholar who opens every layer of meaning. Muslim students in the United Kingdom, United States, Canada, and Australia who want a genuine connection with the Quran need Tafseer, not just translation.

At Kanzulilm International, our online Tafseer course uses the major classical texts — Tafseer Ibn Katheer, Tafseer al-Jalalayn, and Tafseer Bayan ul Quran — taught 1-on-1 via WhatsApp under scholars with verified Ijazah chains. This is authentic Tafseer as it has always been taught — from scholar to student, directly.

What You Will Study

Complete Tafseer Course Syllabus.

Tafseer Ibn Katheer

The most widely studied classical Tafseer in the Sunni tradition by Imam Ibn Katheer (d. 1373 CE). Focuses on explaining the Quran through other Quranic verses, authentic Hadith, and reports from the Companions — providing deep, evidence-based exegesis of every Surah.

Tafseer al-Jalalayn

A concise classical Tafseer by Imam al-Suyuti and Imam al-Mahalli — ideal for students who have foundational Arabic and want a comprehensive but accessible verse-by-verse explanation of the entire Quran within a structured 1-on-1 programme.

Tafseer Bayan ul Quran

The monumental Urdu Tafseer by Mawlana Ashraf Ali Thanwi — the most comprehensive Tafseer in the South Asian tradition. Ideal for students studying in Urdu and those following the Dars-e-Nizami curriculum alongside the online Tafseer course.

Asbab al-Nuzool

The specific occasions that caused each verse to be revealed — understanding Asbab al-Nuzool removes ambiguity from Quranic interpretation and prevents misapplication of verses out of their historical context. Studied as part of every Tafseer session.

Nasikh wa Mansukh

The science of abrogation — understanding which verses modified or replaced earlier rulings, and why. Without Nasikh wa Mansukh, one cannot correctly derive practical Islamic rulings from the Quran or understand the evolution of Islamic law across the revelatory period.

Fiqh Rulings from Quran

Extracting practical Islamic rulings directly from Quranic Ayahs — prayer, fasting, Zakat, marriage, inheritance, business ethics. Students learn how classical scholars used Quranic evidence to establish the Islamic legal framework through Ahkam al-Quran.

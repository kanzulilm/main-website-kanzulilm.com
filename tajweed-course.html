What is Tajweed?

Tajweed is the science of reciting the Holy Quran correctly — pronouncing every letter from its precise articulation point (Makhraj) with its proper characteristics (Sifat), correct elongation (Madd), and appropriate rhythm. The word Tajweed comes from the Arabic root meaning "to make excellent" — and that is exactly what this science does to one's recitation.

Allah commands in the Quran: "Recite the Quran with measured recitation" (73:4). Scholars of all four major Islamic schools of jurisprudence (madhabs) have ruled that observing basic Tajweed rules in recitation is Fard Ayn — individually obligatory upon every Muslim. Reciting without Tajweed can inadvertently alter the meaning of Allah's words.

Without a qualified teacher providing real-time oral correction, it is virtually impossible to master Tajweed from books or videos alone. The tongue learns through listening and being corrected — which is precisely why our online Tajweed class uses 1-on-1 live sessions where the teacher hears every word you recite and corrects instantly.

COMPLETE TAJWEED SYLLABUS

1

Makharij al-Huroof (Articulation Points)

17 precise articulation points across 5 organs — jaw, throat, tongue, lips, nasal cavity

2

Sifat al-Huroof (Letter Characteristics)

Properties of each letter: Qalqalah, Tafkheem, Tarqeeq, Ghunnah, Shiddah, Rakhawah

3

Noon Sakinah & Tanween Rules

Izhar, Idgham (with/without Ghunnah), Iqlab, Ikhfa — 4 essential rules

4

Meem Sakinah Rules

Ikhfa Shafawi, Idgham Shafawi, Izhar Shafawi

5

Madd — Elongation Rules

Madd Tabee'i (natural), Madd Lazim (compulsory), Madd Muttasil, Madd Munfasil and more

6

Waqf & Ibtida (Stopping & Starting)

Where to pause, where not to pause, and how to resume — Waqf Tam, Kafi, Hasan, Qabih

7

Lahn & Applied Recitation

Identifying and eliminating Lahn Jali (obvious errors) and Lahn Khafi (subtle errors)

Why This Matters

Common Tajweed Mistakes
Our Course Corrects.

Most Muslims reciting Quran today were never formally taught Tajweed. These are the most common errors our scholars correct in the free trial session.

Confusing ح and ه

Most non-Arab reciters pronounce ح (Haa) exactly like ه (Ha) — they are entirely different letters from different Makharij. In Surah Al-Fatiha alone, this error occurs 3 times.

Ignoring Ghunnah Duration

Ghunnah must be held for exactly 2 counts. Most reciters either skip it entirely or hold it too long — both are Tajweed errors that affect the beauty and correctness of recitation.

Wrong Madd Length

Madd letters must be elongated for 2, 4, or 6 counts depending on type. Shortening or over-lengthening Madd changes the rhythm and can alter the word's meaning in Arabic.

Stopping at the Wrong Place

Stopping at the wrong Waqf point can completely reverse or destroy the meaning of Quranic verses. For example, stopping between "لا إله" and "إلا الله" creates a statement of disbelief.
